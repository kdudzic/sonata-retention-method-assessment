# Literature survey: diverse, human-like synthetic Polish social-media posts (methods, metrics, model choice)

Scope note: ~45 sources checked via arXiv abstracts/HTML, ACL Anthology, one extracted PDF (PolEval 2025 Śmigiel). "Strong" = peer-reviewed or replicated; "preprint" = arXiv only; "blog" = non-reviewed. Several 2026 IDs (26xx.xxxxx) are recent preprints and should be treated as provisional.

---

## 1. METHODS

### 1a. Verbalized Sampling (VS) — arXiv 2510.01171 (Zhang et al., 2025; Stanford/Northeastern)

**What is known.**
- Thesis: mode collapse is largely caused by *typicality bias* in human preference data (annotators prefer familiar text), not only by the RL algorithm. Fix is prompt-only: ask for a *distribution* of k responses, each with a verbalized probability.
- Exact prompt (from the paper's Fig. 2 and the repo, github.com/CHATS-lab/verbalized-sampling): system prompt "You are a helpful assistant. For each query, please generate a set of five possible responses, each within a separate `<response>` tag. Each `<response>` must include a `<text>` and a numeric `<probability>`. Please sample at random from the tails of the distribution, such that the probability of each response is less than 0.10." The "tail" clause (threshold tau, default 0.10) is the diversity knob: lower tau → more diverse. Variants: VS-CoT (reason first), VS-Multi (multi-turn "give me 5 more"; "confidence" wording worked better than "probability" there). k=5 for creative/synthetic-data tasks, k=20 for open-ended QA; the authors keep k < N because quality degrades at large k.
- Reported gains: semantic diversity (1 − mean pairwise cosine, text-embedding-3-small) up 1.6–2.1x over direct prompting in creative writing; human-rated diversity +25.7%; synthetic math data trained downstream +3–5 points over direct prompting. Quality judged by Claude-3.7-Sonnet.
- **Model-size caveat (important for you):** larger models gain 1.5–2x more diversity from VS than small ones, and VS-Standard *lowers quality on small models*; VS-CoT/VS-Multi mitigate this only on large models. Gains are orthogonal to temperature (tested 0.4–1.4).
- 2026 follow-ups/critiques (all preprints): "Where You Inject Diversity Matters" (arXiv 2606.10302) is the most direct challenge: on Infinity-Chat with GPT-5-mini, VS barely beats direct prompting (LLM-judged diversity 2.84 vs 2.69; embedding diversity 0.23 vs 0.26, i.e. no gain), while "specification-level" injection (first generate diverse keywords/outlines, then condition each output on one spec) reaches 4.87–7.32 with equal quality. They note VS still issues the *same* prompt every time, leaving decoding stochasticity as the only source of variation. Also: "No Single Best Model for Diversity" (arXiv 2604.02319, COLM 2026) confirms generating all answers *in one prompt* elicits the most diverse set vs. repeated single calls; "Anchorless Diversification" (arXiv 2605.30150) finds a single planning call that stratifies semantic directions beats conditioning on previous outputs once full token cost is counted; DIVERGE (arXiv 2602.00238) reports VS combines poorly with RAG; Selective Layer Restoration (arXiv 2602.06665) uses VS as a fixed baseline. No independent replication of the 1.6–2.1x figure on non-English text was found. Self-reported "probabilities" are not calibrated and should not be used as weights.

### 1b. Persona- and attribute-conditioning

- PersonaHub (arXiv 2406.20094): persona prompts drive diversity at scale, but **Kambhatla, Shaib & Govindarajan (arXiv 2505.17390, EMNLP Findings 2025)** measured this: persona prompting raises lexical diversity mainly in *large* models (Llama-3.3-70B, DeepSeek-V3; small 1B/8B Llamas gain little); fine-grained vs coarse personas: no practically significant difference; PersonaHub-derived text is still less diverse than human text on every metric; **an explicit word-length cutoff in the prompt improved diversity more than persona detail** (DeepSeek-V3 self-repetition 0.86 → 0.11). Even with personas, mean embedding cosine among responses was 0.79 — content overlap stays high.
- "More Is Not More" (arXiv 2607.20429, 7 models, factorial design): the first step of persona conditioning gives most of the gain; extra demographic detail does not monotonically help and can hurt; temperature and "be diverse" instructions have negligible effect versus structural interventions.
- "Breaking Homogeneity" (arXiv 2609.30492): *set-level* persona diversification (coverage/dispersion selection, evolutionary generation) nearly doubles persona-induced response separation vs random personas. Implication: how different your 200 persona cards are from each other matters more than how long each card is.
- AttrPrompt (arXiv 2306.15895, NeurIPS 2023; strong): random combinations of attributes (subtopic, length, style) reduce same-class embedding similarity toward real-data levels; fixing any attribute hurts. Successor: Genetic Prompt (arXiv 2509.02040) treats attributes as genes.
- Persona drift over long sequences (dialogue, not post timelines): observer-rated persona expression declines with conversation length (Stable Personas, arXiv 2601.22812); reinjection prompts help (arXiv 2609.24532); but ~50% of failures are *from the outset*, not drift (arXiv 2609.05514). Nothing found that measures drift across a sequence of generated posts specifically.

### 1c. Mode collapse, sampling, batching

- Artificial Hivemind (arXiv 2510.22954, NeurIPS 2025 best paper; strong): with top-p 0.9/T 1.0, mean pairwise embedding similarity among 50 responses exceeds 0.8 for 79% of open-ended queries; min-p (0.1) at T 2.0 still gives 61% of pairs >0.8; inter-model similarity 0.71–0.82 (GPT/Qwen/DeepSeek cluster). English only.
- NoveltyBench (arXiv 2504.05228): 20 models produce far fewer distinct outputs than 8 human writers; **larger models within a family are often less novel than smaller ones**; in-context regeneration (show previous outputs) helps somewhat.
- Preference tuning: with quality-agnostic metrics RLHF models look less diverse, but on "effective semantic diversity" (only quality-passing outputs) they look more diverse; larger models have more effective diversity, smaller ones more unique content per token budget (arXiv 2504.12522).
- Sampling: min-p (arXiv 2407.01082, ICLR 2025) claims creative diversity at high T, but "Turning Down the Heat" (arXiv 2506.13681) finds the evidence does not support the quality/diversity claims. "Sampling More, Getting Less" (arXiv 2605.11128, 14 models) argues collapse is an order/shape miscalibration of the per-step distribution, so truncation heuristics cannot fix it. "Where You Inject Diversity" shows T=1.5 barely moves diversity on Qwen3-4B/DeepSeek-V3.2 but drops quality. Net: temperature/min-p are weak levers; prompt-level structure is the strong lever. Note: OpenRouter exposes min-p only for some providers.
- Batching: one call producing many candidates beats N single calls (VS; arXiv 2604.02319), but very long batches degrade distributional fidelity (arXiv 2601.05414). Anchored regeneration (showing previous outputs) raises final-pool diversity but costs tokens (arXiv 2605.30150).

### 1d. Draft-then-humanize, informal register, typos

- Base vs instruct: GPTZero/Pangram often label *base-model* text as human while flagging the instruction-tuned sibling; HIP (iterative paraphrasing with a lightly tuned base model) improves "human-likeness" from 0.6B to 70B (arXiv 2605.19516, preprint). MASH (arXiv 2601.08564): SFT + DPO + inference refinement for style humanization, 92% attack success vs black-box detectors. Adversarial Paraphrasing (arXiv 2506.07001), DAMAGE (arXiv 2501.03437: 19 humanizer tools; many detectors fail, but augmentation-trained detectors recover), TH-Bench (arXiv 2503.08708). All measure *detector evasion*, not human-judged naturalness.
- Typos/noise: a spectral-detection preprint (arXiv 2608.25944 family of results, reported second-hand) describes a paradox: typo perturbations make text "less human yet less detectable"; fine-tuned detectors are brittle to typos. No paper shows typo injection makes text *rated more human by people*. Treat typos as a detector-evasion hack, not a naturalness method; use sparingly and persona-consistently.
- Register: instruction-tuned models write denser, more academic prose than humans even when prompted for fiction/TV scripts (Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025, arXiv 2410.16107; strong). Key Biber-feature differences persist "even when prompts ask for human-like output" and chat models cluster together stylistically; model matters more than decoding (arXiv 2604.14111). Replicated in Czech (AI-Koditex, arXiv 2509.10179): system prompt changes style, temperature does not. SAE work finds a small language-agnostic informal-register core steerable across languages (arXiv 2603.26236) — not usable via API, but evidence that "informality" is a real controllable direction.
- Social-media-specific: "When Detection Fails" (arXiv 2506.09975, ACL Findings 2025): 505k generated posts; prompted base/closed models produce *longer* posts, lower TTR, over-generate hashtags and emojis, no @mentions/links; fine-tuning on real tweets dramatically reduces Biber-feature gaps and defeats detectors; human study confirms. "Are LLM-powered social media bots realistic?" (arXiv 2508.00998, GPT-4.1-mini, T=0): generated tweets have far less negative *and* positive sentiment, near-zero expletives/abusive terms, lower reading difficulty, 4x more hashtags, more 1st-person pronouns. Reddit mimicry with comment history (arXiv 2608.28989): structure/formality matched well, emotion off (understated joy, overstated anger). Machine text on Reddit shows assistant-like "warmth/status" signals yet gets comparable engagement (arXiv 2510.07226). Simulated Reddit politics (arXiv 2506.21620): humans could barely tell comments apart, yet embeddings separated them cleanly — a warning that LLM-judge naturalness and embedding tests disagree.
- Persona corpora: Synthia (arXiv 2507.14922, ACL 2026) grounds personas in 170M Bluesky posts; BluePrint (arXiv 2510.02343) evaluates "stylistic realism" at persona-cluster and population level.

### 1e. AI-isms / fingerprints

- Em-dash: 0.0 (Llama) to 9.1 per 1k words (GPT-4.1) even under "no markdown" instructions; explicit "avoid em dashes" fails on some models; present pre-RLHF (arXiv 2603.27006, single-author preprint). SV-Detect (arXiv 2606.07313): a regex bank of em-dashes, formal connectives, markdown already carries substantial AUROC. Excess vocabulary ("delves", "underscores", "showcasing") in 15M PubMed abstracts (Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025, arXiv 2406.07016; strong, English). Model fingerprints survive style prompts (arXiv 2503.01659). "Measuring AI Slop" (arXiv 2509.19163) gives a taxonomy (templatedness, repetition, coherence/relevance). **No Polish-specific AI-ism lexicon was found.** Closest: Bielik v3's 200-feature stylometric filter (hapax ratio, looping indicator, diacritic counts, replacement-character ratio; arXiv 2505.02550) and PolEval Śmigiel's qualitative finding that the most detectable Polish MGT repeats principal nouns/phrases and grammatical patterns, especially under greedy decoding; multinomial sampling produced the most human-like Polish text.

**Recommendation (methods).** Implement, per author: (1) a *spec-first* pipeline — one planning call per author producing N distinct "post seeds" (occasion + mood + length bucket + punctuation habit + which tic fires), then one generation call per seed; (2) VS-style batch (k=5–8, tau 0.10) *within* each author as a baseline; (3) AttrPrompt-style random attribute sampling (length 12/25/45 words, sentiment incl. negative/annoyed, has-typo, has-emoji, lowercase, etc.) with a hard word-length cutoff in the prompt (cheapest proven diversity lever); (4) a rewrite stage only if you measure that it helps — ask for "same content, said the way this person would type it on a phone", not "add typos". Avoid relying on temperature/min-p. Make persona cards *maximally dispersed across the set* (embed cards, reject near-duplicates). Keep posts short — length is the biggest confound in every metric and detector.

**Open uncertainties.** No evidence any of these were tested in Polish; VS gains on cheap models may be nil or negative; spec-first results are one 2026 preprint; nobody has measured persona drift over post timelines.

## 2. METRICS

**What is standard (2025–26 synthetic-data papers).**
- Lexical: Shaib et al. (arXiv 2403.00553, IJCNLP-AACL 2025 demo; `diversity` PyPI package) show compression ratio (gzip) captures the whole TTR/MATTR/MTLD family and recommend reporting **compression ratio + self-repetition of long n-grams + Self-BLEU** (low mutual correlation); all are length-confounded. Used verbatim by arXiv 2505.17390 (CR, CR-POS, NDS, self-repetition, BERTScore homogenization). Distinct-n/Self-BLEU remain ubiquitous but a meta-evaluation (arXiv 2506.00514) shows Self-BLEU-3 can rate a set of near-paraphrases as *more* diverse; embedding Vendi aligned best with meaningful diversity.
- Semantic: mean pairwise cosine (VS, Hivemind, AttrPrompt), Vendi score (arXiv 2210.02410; effective number of unique items), DCScore (arXiv 2502.08512; classification-based, cheaper, axiom-satisfying), NoveltyBench distinct-k (equivalence classes via judge). A practitioner meta-analysis (J. Niklaus, X post, 83 pretraining runs; blog-level) found most diversity scores don't predict downstream quality; G-Vendi (gradient entropy, arXiv 2505.20161, NeurIPS 2025) did — but that is for reasoning data, not style corpora.
- Human-likeness: (a) detector AUC — Binoculars (arXiv 2401.12070) is the standard zero-shot score, but Luminol-AIDetect (arXiv 2604.25860) reports zero-shot methods collapse on diacritic-rich languages incl. Polish on MULTITuDE, and short texts are the hardest regime (Short-PHD, arXiv 2504.02873: 50-token AUC ~0.63 for PHD; Śmigiel: social media at 36 words was the hardest in-domain genre). In Polish specifically, the Śmigiel-calibrated Binoculars with Bielik and a character-level perplexity-difference method (Gemma-3-27B vs PLLuM-12B, 81% on unseen generators; aclanthology 2025.poleval-main.5) are the available zero-shot options; supervised detectors are best for Central European languages (CEAID, arXiv 2509.26051). (b) LLM-as-judge: GPTZero beat Claude Opus-4.6 (77.9%) and GPT-5.5 (75.9%) on human-vs-AI dialogue, and LLM judges are *fooled by persona prompting* (Inverse Turing Bench, arXiv 2606.21844) — i.e., your pipeline will inflate judge scores. Self-detection is weak (arXiv 2502.12743). (c) Perplexity: Śmigiel shows MGT has consistently lower perplexity than Polish HWT; DivEye (arXiv 2509.18880) uses *variance* of surprisal as the human signal. (d) Feature-level: Biber/stylometric comparison against a real corpus (Reinhart; Dawkins) is the most interpretable and is where social-media LLM text fails (sentiment, expletives, length, hashtags, pronouns).

**Recommendation (metrics).** Always compute against a real Polish reference sample of the same length distribution (TwitterEmo-PL, 36k tweets, or PolEval CBD/Śmigiel social-media HWT), and report the *gap to reference*, not raw values. Stratify every metric by length bucket.

**Open uncertainties.** No validated Polish AI-ism lexicon; multilingual embedding choice (e.g. BGE-M3/multilingual-e5 via PL-MTEB) affects cosine-based scores; LLM-judge naturalness is unreliable for persona text.

## 3. MODEL CHOICE

**What is known.**
- Diversity vs size is non-monotonic: NoveltyBench says larger = often *less* novel; arXiv 2504.12522 says larger = more *effective* diversity; epistemic diversity falls with size (arXiv 2510.04226); "No Single Best Model" (18 models) finds the best diversity model varies by prompt and a router beats any single model (26.3 vs 23.8). Generator *ensembles* mitigate distribution collapse (arXiv 2511.01490). VS and persona prompting both pay off mainly on large models (2510.01171; 2505.17390). Śmigiel: Polish text from medium (11–12B) generators was hardest to detect — "size is not everything".
- Polish quality: PLCC (arXiv 2503.00995, 600 Qs; Gemini-Exp 83%, best open ~12 pts lower; aggregator 2026: Gemini-3.1-Pro ~97, GPT-5 ~89.5; GPT-OSS-20B 32 vs 120B 54 — a steep scale effect). LLMzSzŁ (arXiv 2501.02266): Mistral-Large 67, Bielik-11B 57.5. Open PL LLM Leaderboard (SpeakLeash, HF) covers open models only; PLLuM excluded for contamination (arXiv 2505.02550). Jeleśniański's Feb-2026 human-rated report (12 chatbots, 11 raters, blog-level but the only 2026 commercial comparison): overall Gemini 3.1 Pro > Qwen 3.5 Plus ≈ Llama 4 > GPT-5.2 > Grok 4.2 > Claude Sonnet 4.6; on the Polish-correctness task Llama 4 scored highest, commercial models just behind, EuroLLM > Bielik 3.0 > PLLuM; Opus 4.6 clearly beat Sonnet 4.6 in a play-off. No small/cheap tier was tested. Multilingual IFEval variants (Marco-Bench-MIF, arXiv 2507.11882) show 25–35 pt gaps for lower-resource languages but Polish coverage unconfirmed; no Polish stylistic-constraint benchmark exists.

**Recommendation.** Benchmark 3 tiers on OpenRouter: one cheap (e.g. Gemini Flash-class / Qwen 3.5 mid / Llama 4 Maverick), one mid (DeepSeek-V3.2 or Mistral Large 3), one flagship (Gemini 3.1 Pro or Claude Opus/GPT-5.x). Expect cheap models to be *stylistically adequate* for 12–50-word Polish posts but to gain little from VS and to drift into assistant register; expect flagship to gain more from structured prompting. Consider mixing 2 generators across authors to break inter-model homogeneity (Hivemind 0.71–0.82 inter-model similarity).

**Open uncertainties.** No published comparison of cheap vs flagship on Polish *informal* text; Jeleśniański's ranking is one person's panel; Bielik/PLLuM are not on OpenRouter-class APIs at scale.

## PRIORITIZED METHODS TO BENCHMARK (6)
1. **Spec-first two-stage** (per-author planning call → per-seed generation) — strongest 2026 evidence (2606.10302, 2605.30150).
2. **VS batch (k=5–8, tau=0.10)** per author — cheap, well-known baseline; test on both tiers because small-model gains may vanish.
3. **AttrPrompt random attributes + hard length cutoff** (length, sentiment incl. negative, device/typing habit, emoji/hashtag flag) — the length cutoff alone is the best-supported single lever (2505.17390).
4. **Dispersed persona set** (reject near-duplicate cards; evolutionary/coverage selection, 2609.30492) — orthogonal to 1–3.
5. **Draft → "retype as this person on a phone"** rewrite (not "add typos") — test whether it closes the Biber/sentiment gap; measure, don't assume.
6. **Two-generator mix across authors** (cheap + flagship) — targets inter-model homogeneity; also gives your cost comparison for free.
(Deprioritize: temperature/min-p sweeps; "be diverse" instructions.)

## METRICS TO COMPUTE (8), each vs. a length-matched real Polish reference
1. Compression ratio (gzip) + self-repetition of 4-grams (Shaib `diversity` package), within-author and corpus-wide.
2. Self-BLEU-4 and distinct-2 (for comparability with the literature only).
3. Mean pairwise cosine (multilingual embedding) + Vendi score on embeddings; report share of pairs >0.8 (Hivemind-style) per author.
4. Near-duplicate rate (MinHash on normalized 3-grams, threshold ~0.8) and exact-dup rate.
5. Binoculars (Bielik or PLLuM pair, Śmigiel-calibrated) score distribution vs reference, plus surprisal variance (DivEye-style).
6. Supervised detector AUC: train a small classifier (e.g. HerBERT) on your posts vs reference HWT; AUC near 0.5 is the target — most honest naturalness test for Polish short text.
7. Feature-gap panel: length, TTR, hashtag/emoji/@mention rate, expletive rate, sentiment polarity distribution, em-dash/ellipsis/all-caps/no-capitalization rates, top-20 over-represented lemmas (your Polish AI-ism lexicon, derived empirically à la Kobak).
8. LLM-judge pairwise human-vs-synthetic (flagship judge, blind) — report, but treat as upper bound given persona-prompt vulnerability (2606.21844).
